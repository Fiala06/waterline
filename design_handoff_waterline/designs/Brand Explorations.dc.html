Turn 1 · Name + logo
Five names, five marks
Descriptive and water-chemistry names. The marks are simple geometric shapes so they stay legible as a 16px favicon and a home-screen icon. The screens use 1a Waterline as the working name.
1a · Recommended
Waterline
A tank seen from the side: a water level in a circular gauge. It suggests measurement and is easy to say. Works for reef and freshwater tanks alike.
1b
Titrate
A test vial and a drop, the drop-count test most keepers know. The most lab-like option. It works well as a verb (“titrate before you dose”) but leans toward water testing more than the full log.
1c
Cycle
The nitrogen cycle as three nodes on a ring: ammonia, nitrite, nitrate. Short and memorable. Hobbyists will get the reference, though the word alone is generic, so the domain and search may be crowded.
1d
Tankbook
A notebook that is also a tank: a spine on the left and water filling the page. The most descriptive name, closest to the hand-written log it replaces. Friendly and plain, with the least field-journal character.
1e
Tidemark
A tidemark is the line left where water once stood, a record of levels over time. Three receding bars read as history, or as a bar chart. The most distinctive name, and it suits reef keepers especially well.
